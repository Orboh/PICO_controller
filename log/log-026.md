# Log 026 — What guides the robot (hand pose, not the human elbow); wrist and right-arm plan (2026-11-01)

Final version after the Elephant and Goldfish reviews (draft replaced).

## Short answer (plain words)
1. The PICO only knows where each **controller** is and how it is turned. It does not know your elbow
   or shoulder angles.
2. The program **commands** the robot's gripper toward the controller's movement since you squeezed:
   half the distance (hand 10 cm → gripper 5 cm). With wrist mode (`--orientation`) it also commands the
   **same turn** (10° hand → 10° gripper).
3. The robot has 7 joints; placing and turning a gripper needs only 6, so the robot's **elbow is chosen
   by the program**, not copied from your elbow. Without wrist mode (all pilot data so far) the gripper's
   turn is not controlled at all; it changes as a side effect.
4. We do not measure what the real arm did (no feedback from the arm yet); we know only what we sent.

## How it is converted (code)
| Step | Where |
|---|---|
| headset axes → robot axes | `pico_teleop/frames.py:17` (R_ROBOT_FROM_XR), `:38` |
| at the squeeze: store hand pose and gripper pose ("anchors") | `controller.py:295-303` |
| position target = anchor gripper + 0.5 × (hand − anchor hand), max 0.25 m/s | `controller.py:342-346` |
| turn target (wrist mode) = hand turn since the squeeze, applied 1:1 in the robot frame, max 90°/s | `controller.py:350-356` |
| joints from the target: damped least squares, started from the previous answer | `kinematics.py:97-127` (position), `:134` (position + turn) |
| limit avoidance (steer joints away from their stops) | **position-only mode only** (`controller.py:381-382`); off in wrist mode |
| checks: speed, joint limits (3° guard), drift, singularity (position part only), posture 60° | `controller.py:384-405` |

What the operator will notice in wrist mode: the gripper may keep turning ~0.5 s after the hand stops (90°/s);
a hand turn > 40° ahead of the gripper → FAULT "turn slower"; > 30° in one update or > 720°/s → FAULT; a turn
the arm cannot do → it follows the position only, FAULT after 0.5 s with a gap > 20°; wrist turns count toward
the 60° posture limit.

## What is widely used
| Method | Examples | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Leader arm, joint 1:1 | ALOHA, GELLO, our leader arm | no IK; needs a second arm |
| Controller pose → IK, grip as clutch (**ours**) | NVIDIA Isaac Teleop / LeRobot (Placo IK, clutch re-centring, soft orientation), XRoboToolkit | most common VR method; the elbow is left to the IK |
| + elbow trackers / "swivel angle" | XRoboToolkit paper (elbow trackers as IK constraints), SEW-Mimic (2026) | fixes the free elbow of 7-joint arms |
| Whole-body retargeting | humanoids (Valkyrie VR) | posture tasks with weights |

Sources: nvidia.github.io/IsaacTeleop (lerobot devices), huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/isaac_teleop,
arxiv.org/pdf/2508.00097 (XRoboToolkit), arxiv.org/html/2602.01632v1 (SEW-Mimic), arxiv.org/abs/2307.13122
(swivel angle), arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07534v1 (Valkyrie). From the papers/docs, not checked in code.
Known weakness of hand-only control: the elbow is free, so the arm's posture can differ from the leader demos.
